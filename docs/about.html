RELATIVE LAND PRODUCTIVITY / ABOUT
Map Methods

About this map

A public web map that shows, field by field across Brazil, where crop and pasture productivity rose or fell between the 2020 and 2024 agricultural years, broken out by crop type, with a suggested land use decision per field.

Purpose

The map is meant to say, at the field level, which land use decision could be improved: restore the land, switch the crop, change the land use pattern, abandon the field, intensify, or improve yields on the same crop.

Productivity is measured from the satellite greenness record over the growing season, with the growing season taken from CONAB crop calendars per crop and state. The measure corrects for rainfall and temperature changes that move greenness without moving yield. The quantity on the map is relative productivity: a field against the median of its peers in the same crop and region, compared between 2020 and 2024.

The suggested decisions are screening results from satellite greenness. They are not agronomic advice. The method does not know prices, soils, tenure, inputs or the farmer's plans, and a field flagged here deserves a visit before any decision is taken on it.

What this release holds

24,554,482 Trazo4 fields in all 27 states, 190.7 million hectares, scored for the agricultural years 2020 (1 September 2019 to 31 August 2020) and 2024 (1 September 2023 to 31 August 2024), plus 748,022 fields from the 2020 delineation whose land cover in 2024 is no longer a crop or pasture class, shown as abandoned. The tiles were built on 12 November 2026.

The greenness source is MODIS MOD13Q1, a 250 m 16-day NDVI product. 78 percent of Brazilian fields are smaller than one MODIS pixel, so 20,344,905 fields (82.9 percent) are marked low confidence: their greenness is shared with neighboring land. The Where card on the map can fade or hide them. 375,000 fields (1.5 percent) have no data because too few clear composites were observed in one of the years; Pará holds 256,202 of them (20.7 percent of its fields) and in Amapá 59.6 percent of fields have no data. A Sentinel-2 run at 30 m is being extracted for Mato Grosso and the other tier 1 states (Pará, Amazonas, Acre, Rondônia, Roraima, Amapá) and will replace the MODIS values there in a later release.

Pasture vigor from MapBiomas is not in this release. No public GeoTIFF of the product was found, so pasture is scored on NDVI alone and the vigor view is left off the map.

The weather correction is weak (median R squared 0.15 across the 134 fitted crop by state pairs) and the municipal yield validation is weak (Spearman 0.06 to 0.18 for soybean against IBGE yields). The methods page states both and why.

Reading the map

Below zoom 9 the map shows H3 hexagon cells with the mean, the net share or the most common value of the fields inside, at a cell size that follows the zoom: about 40 km across when fully zoomed out, 15 km at the national view and 6 km (36 km²) from a state view until the fields appear; from zoom 9 the fields themselves. In the change view a cell is colored by how many more of its fields declined than gained, and cells where the two balance are faded, so the regions where productivity fell against peers stand out. From zoom 9 to 11 the smallest fields are merged into their neighbors so the tiles stay small; from zoom 12 every field is drawn. Clicking a field opens a card with its values in both years, its confidence and its suggested decision. The state selector flies to a state and switches the counts in the legend to that state.

Code and data

The site, the pipeline and the methods text are in the repository at github.com/tristangrupp/relative-land-productivity. The field tiles (6.4 GB), the summary tiles (53 MB) and the statistics file are served as PMTiles and JSON from a public bucket under s3://wri-datalab-sandbox/trazofields/trazo4/relative-land-productivity/tiles/; the file docs/DATA_CONTRACT.md lists every attribute the map reads.
