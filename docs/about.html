Map Methods

About this map

Explore how relative productivity changed for crops and pasture across Brazil from 2020 to 2024. The map suggests land use decisions for areas and individual fields.

Purpose

The map compares crops and pasture in each 6 km area with the same uses across Brazil. For example, “Good land underused” means crops are doing well while pasture lags. The map then compares each field with nearby fields of the same crop. A field below its peers may be a candidate for assisted natural regeneration if native plants are returning, or for restoration if large native patches are nearby. Otherwise, raising yields could spare land elsewhere. Each field card shows one suggested action and other options. Fields smaller than a satellite pixel are marked too small to judge.

The map uses satellite greenness during the growing season, as defined by CONAB calendars for each crop and state. It adjusts for rainfall and temperature, then compares each field with the median for nearby fields of the same crop in 2020 and 2024.

Use these suggestions to decide where to investigate. Satellite greenness gives an initial indication. Visit the field and consider soils, input costs, land tenure, prices, and the farmer’s plans before deciding.

What this release holds

The release covers 24,554,482 Trazo4 fields across all 27 states, totaling 190.7 million hectares. The agricultural years run from 1 September 2019 to 31 August 2020 and from 1 September 2023 to 31 August 2024. The map also shows 748,022 fields from 2020 as abandoned because MapBiomas shows neither crop nor pasture there in 2024. The original tiles were built on 12 November 2026.

Most states use MODIS MOD13Q1, a 16-day greenness index (NDVI) at 250 m. A MODIS pixel is larger than 78 percent of Brazil’s fields, so readings from many fields include neighboring land. Mato Grosso switched to 30 m Sentinel-2 on 14 November 2026. Pará, Rondônia and Acre switched on 17 November, Amazonas, Roraima and Amapá on 18 November, Mato Grosso do Sul on 19 November, and Goiás and Tocantins on 20 November, and Maranhão on 21 November. With those eleven states on Sentinel-2, 17,302,378 fields (70.5 percent) have low confidence, and the decision view marks them “too small to judge yet.” Another 112,636 fields receive no suggestion. In Amapá, 59.6 percent of fields lacked enough clear MODIS images; on Sentinel-2, 1.2 percent do. The field card and tooltip show the satellite used. Next are the other 15 states and the Federal District.

Pasture scores use NDVI alone. The MapBiomas pasture vigor data was unavailable as a public GeoTIFF.

The weather model explains little of the variation (median R squared of 0.15 across 134 crop and state pairs). Adjusted soybean scores have weak correlations with IBGE municipal yields (Spearman 0.06 to 0.18). See the methods page for details.

Reading the map

Below zoom 9, the map groups fields into H3 hexagons about 6 km across (36 km²). The change view compares how crop greenness changed in each cell with the change across Brazil. Gray cells kept pace. The decision view colors a cell when a suggested action stands out. At zoom 9, the map switches to field outlines. At zooms 9 to 11, the smallest fields are merged into neighbors to reduce tile size. Every field appears from zoom 12. Click a field to see its scores, confidence and suggested action. Select a state to zoom to it and see its legend counts.
