Methods

How field productivity is measured from satellite greenness, how weather is corrected for, how the change classes and suggested decisions are derived, and what the measure cannot say.

This release measures greenness with MODIS MOD13Q1, a 250 m 16-day NDVI product. 78 percent of Brazilian fields are smaller than one MODIS pixel, so most field values are shared with neighboring land and are marked low confidence on the map. A Sentinel-2 run at 30 m is being extracted for Mato Grosso and the other tier 1 states and will replace the MODIS values there. The full specification is methods/METHODS.md and the implementation notes are pipeline/ANALYZE.md in the repository.

No Google Earth Engine was used. Every raster was downloaded or streamed and reduced to fields on one machine with coverage-fraction weights (the share of each pixel inside each field), computed once with exactextract and applied to every composite as a sparse matrix product.

2. The agricultural year

Agricultural year Y runs from 1 September of Y-1 to 31 August of Y. "2020" is 1 September 2019 to 31 August 2020 and "2024" is 1 September 2023 to 31 August 2024. Starting on 1 September keeps the September soybean sowing in Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Paraná, Santa Catarina and São Paulo inside the window, and the window still holds the second-season maize harvest, which ends in August in the main states. The year also matches MapBiomas, which labels a temporary crop by the first cycle whose peak falls around January to February of Y.

The 2020 and 2024 field polygons are different delineations of the same land. Every metric is computed on the 2024 polygons for both years, so the change is measured on one outline. The 2020 crop of a 2024 polygon is the modal MapBiomas 2020 class inside it, which makes a crop switch visible per field. The 2020 polygons are also scored for 2020, and those whose MapBiomas 2024 class is not a crop or pasture class are shown as abandoned.

3. The growing season window

Each field's window is the state by crop window from the CONAB calendar, sowing start to harvest end, in data/calendars/class_window_lookup.csv. A window is a run of months in the September to August order, so October to April is one run that wraps the calendar year. MapBiomas temporary crop classes 19 and 41 share a window and are pooled as one peer class. Perennial classes (sugarcane, coffee, citrus, oil palm, other perennial, pasture, forest plantation) take the full year. Forest plantation and unspecified agriculture are not in the calendar table and take the full year too.

4. Greenness per field and the seasonal integral

Per composite, a MODIS pixel is masked when its pixel reliability is 2 or 3 (snow, ice or cloud) or its quality flag is 2 or 3. The field value of a composite is the coverage-weighted mean of its valid pixels. A composite is missing for a field when under half of its covered area is valid; missing composites are filled by linear interpolation in time, and the count of observed composites is kept as n_obs. The Terra composite for day 225 of 2024 does not exist on the Planetary Computer and is interpolated for every field.

Baseline: for annual crops, the field's own minimum NDVI over the 23 composites, its bare-soil floor. For perennial classes, which have no bare month, the 5th percentile of all fields of that class in the state and year.

The headline metric is the seasonal integral iNDVI = sum over the window of max(NDVI - baseline, 0), with each 16-day composite weighted 16 / 30.4375 months, so the unit is NDVI-months. Peak NDVI, the timing of the peak and the full-year integral are kept as diagnostics. The integral sums green canopy over the season, the quantity that light-use-efficiency and crop models relate to biomass and yield, and it ranks dense fields better than the peak alone, because NDVI saturates on dense canopies and the integral also counts duration. The references are in section 10 of METHODS.md.

Check on Mato Grosso 2024: soybean (116,914 fields) peaks a median 4.8 months after 1 September, in late January, with median peak NDVI 0.884 and a window integral of 2.45 NDVI-months; pasture (948,056 fields) has a full-year integral of 2.88 above its floor of 0.402.

5. Peer-relative productivity and the weather correction

Weather moves NDVI without moving a field's standing against its neighbors. Two layers remove it.

Peer region. The H3 resolution 5 cell (about 253 km², 17 km across) of the field centroid. A peer group is (cell, peer class, year) with at least 30 fields whose class is agricultural and that have a raw value for at least half of their window composites; a smaller group falls back to the resolution 4 cell (1,770 km²), then resolution 3. Peer cells pool across state borders. Municipalities are not used as peers because their area ranges from under 100 km² to 159,000 km².

Layer 1, peer-relative metric. R = (iNDVI - median of the group) / (1.4826 x MAD of the group), in robust peer standard deviations.

Layer 2, regression on weather anomalies. Per peer class and state, the group medians of both years are regressed on the standardized precipitation and temperature anomalies of the window with quadratic terms. The anomaly is against the mean and standard deviation of the nine complete agricultural years 2016 to 2024 (the rasters start in January 2015, so 2015 is incomplete). The fitted weather term is removed at field level from the field's own anomalies, with three guards: a class by state is fitted only with at least 30 groups spanning both years, field anomalies are clipped to the range of group anomalies the fit saw, and the term is capped at half the intercept. 134 of the 176 class by state pairs were fitted. The median R squared of the fits is 0.15 (quartiles 0.07 and 0.24, maximum 0.57), which is weak: the regional weather anomaly explains little of the variation in group medians, and most of the correction comes from layer 1. The coefficients, R squared and anomaly ranges per pair are in data/summaries/weather_fit.csv.

Layer 1 is recomputed on the adjusted metric, giving r_adj in peer standard deviations. The scale the map shows is prod = adjusted metric / peer median of the adjusted metric, 1.0 at the peer median, clipped to 0 to 5.

In 2024 the windows had a median precipitation anomaly of -1.6 standard deviations and a temperature anomaly of +1.6 in Mato Grosso, the 2023 to 2024 El Niño drought. A peer-relative measure cancels weather that is common to a cell, but it cannot separate irrigation (a pivot in a drought year scores a gain that is water, not management), a shift in sowing date, a cultivar or management change, or a crop switch inside the same MapBiomas class.

6. Change classes

D = r_adj 2024 - r_adj 2020 on the 2024 polygon, in peer standard deviations (SD). The number the map shows beside the class is a different quantity: change = prod 2024 - prod 2020 on the ratio scale. The summary cells below zoom 9 color the share of a cell's fields that declined minus the share that gained, in steps of 5 and 15 percentage points; the mean ratio-scale change of the cell is in its tooltip and card. The mean sits within 0.08 of zero in 82 percent of cells, so a map of it is flat, while the net share shows where decline outnumbers gain.

7. Suggested decisions

The suggestion has two tiers, set by the user on 13 November 2026. The area call comes first, per H3 cell of about 15 km (resolution 5). The field call comes second, inside the area. The field's record is judged against nearby fields of the same crop; the area is judged against the same uses across Brazil, with the RESOLVE 2017 ecoregion shown as context. The national reference follows the user's request of the same day: “lets do brazil as a whole then to get the true frontiers of productivity change”.

Area call

For each cell and each crop class, the median weather-adjusted greenness of the class in the cell is compared with the national median of the class, in national standard deviations (SD) of cell medians. The classes are pooled, weighted by field count, into crops (soybean, other temporary crops, sugarcane, rice, cotton, coffee, citrus, other perennials, oil palm) and pasture. A class needs 30 fields in the cell. Mosaic of uses is shown in the cell card but does not enter the call. Below means more than 0.75 SD under the national median; declining means the gap grew by more than 0.4 SD from 2020 to 2024. These put 24 percent of pasture cells below and 19 percent declining, about the lowest quarter and fifth; crop cells sit mostly above the national crop medians, so only 6 percent are below.

Version 1, which judged each field alone, called 1,262,572 fields on 2024 outlines restore (below their neighbors and declining). Version 2 calls 97.2 percent of them improve yields, because their areas are not weak and declining for both crops and pasture; restore falls to 91,076. Version 1's change land use pattern became switch paid off (1,669,629) or switch failed (671,801), and its switch crop (4,675,213) became mostly improve yields. The full cross table is in data/summaries/decisions_v1_vs_v2.csv and the report in pipeline/DECISIONS_V2.md.

Before the area call was used, the weather term was refitted per ecoregion and crop with each location's mean annual rainfall as an extra input. R squared rose from 0.15 to 0.19, but the blocks of 50 to 200 km in the 2024 change of the area term did not shrink, so the original fit stays. Part of an area-declining call may be weather the adjustment missed.

These are screening suggestions from satellite greenness, not agronomic advice. The method does not know prices, soils, tenure, inputs or the farmer's plans, and every suggestion needs a field visit before any decision.

8. Validation

Field metrics were aggregated to municipality means weighted by area for soybean (MapBiomas 39), maize (classes 19 and 41 in states where maize dominates) and sugarcane (20), and compared with IBGE municipal yields for 2020 and 2024. The national results in data/summaries/validation.csv are Spearman correlations between the municipal mean and yield.

The correlations of the 2024 minus 2020 differences with the yield differences are 0.03 (maize), -0.02 (soybean) and 0.08 (sugarcane) for r_adj. A development run on two states gave Spearman 0.38 for maize and 0.28 for soybean.

Verdict: the raw integral correlates moderately with the IBGE yields only for soybean in 2020 (0.46) and sugarcane in 2024 (0.48), and not for maize. prod, r_adj and change correlate below 0.24 everywhere, so the relative productivity and the change on the map are not yield estimates. They rank a field against its neighbors; they do not predict how much it produced. The literature expects 0.5 to 0.8 for levels from fine-resolution greenness, and this release does not reach that. Two reasons are built in: MODIS pixels are larger than most fields, so the municipal mean mixes field and non-field greenness, and the relative metric removes the regional level by construction, so a municipal mean of it should not track absolute yields across municipalities. The Sentinel-2 run is the planned fix for the first reason.

9. Limits of this release

  • Pixel size. MODIS pixels are 250 m (6.25 ha). 78 percent of fields are smaller than one pixel and read one or two cells shared with neighbors. A field is marked low confidence when it is under 6.25 ha or fewer than 75 percent of its window composites were observed in either year; 20,344,905 of the 24,554,482 fields (83 percent) are low confidence nationally, 684,199 of 1,151,506 in Mato Grosso. The map can fade or hide them.
  • Missing composite. The Terra composite for day 225 of 2024 is absent from the Planetary Computer and is interpolated for every field.
  • Wet-season gaps. In Pará the wet-season composites are valid for 31 to 45 percent of fields; fields with fewer than half of their window composites observed get no relative value and fall in the no data class (256,202 of 1,238,196 fields in Pará).
  • Pasture vigor is not implemented. The brief asked for the MapBiomas Collection 10 pasture vigor classes as a second attribute for pasture fields. No public GeoTIFF of that product was found, so the vigor attributes are null, the vigor view is left off the map, and pasture is scored on NDVI alone.
  • Weak weather fit. The layer 2 regressions explain a median 15 percent of the variation in group medians, so most of the weather correction is the peer-relative layer.
  • Validation. Municipal yield correlations are weak (section 8).
  • Mixed classes. The temporary crop classes 19 and 41 mix crops; a field that changed from maize to beans inside class 41 looks like a productivity change. Sugarcane harvest date moves the integral.
  • Outlines. The 2020 metric on a 2024 polygon can average two 2020 fields. The drawn outlines are simplified with a one-pixel tolerance (0.0001 degree); the area shown is the original.
  • Low zooms. From zoom 9 to 11 the smallest fields are merged into their neighbors so the tiles stay small; from zoom 12 upward every field is drawn.
